# SmallPages
[![GPL v3 or later](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-GPLv3%20or%20later-lightgrey.svg)](LICENSE)

Single-purpose browser tools. Each one is a standalone HTML file — open it, use it,
close it. Nothing is uploaded anywhere; every image, GIF and byte stays in your tab.

**Live:** https://9-7-8.github.io/SmallPages/

| [`dragon-tree.html`](dragon-tree.html) | Drop a folder of saved Flight Rising dragon pages and get the family tree out of them: parents above children, each dragon with its own sprite and a name plate edged in its colour, founders marked with an egg. A lineage gives its founders a colour and it runs down the tree, so a dragon descended from two lineages comes out the midpoint of both &mdash; averaged in CIELAB, because the midpoint of two colours in RGB is usually darker and duller than either. Saves as an SVG with the sprites embedded, as a PNG, or as the table of dragons in CSV. Nothing is fetched from Flight Rising; it reads the HTML you already saved, with the browser's own parser. Ported from `dragontree.py`, which imported a function for CIEDE2000 colour difference and never called it, worked out how big each picture should be and then drew every one at twenty by ten inches at 300 dpi regardless, gave up silently above sixty-four dragons, and placed each parent one row above its *nearest* child &mdash; which sends an edge pointing upward as soon as a dragon has children a generation apart. Here a dragon sits one row below its lowest parent, and a test walks every edge to prove none of them points the wrong way. |
| [`games/gridlock/`](games/gridlock/) | A traffic puzzle, and a knock-off of a knock-off. Coloured blobs make cars and blobs of the same colour eat them; you draw the one-way roads in between and try to get a set number home without the whole thing seizing up. A cell holds one car per lane, so a stretch of two-way road carries one each way and a junction &mdash; any cell roads arrive at from directions that are not opposite &mdash; holds exactly one, whichever way it came. That one rule is the game: a junction nobody is directing makes every car creep across it while another arm waits, and carries about half what it should. Later levels hand you the three answers to that and make you pick. A stop sign goes on the approach and gives the other arm the junction at full speed, at the cost of starving its own if the gaps run out. Lights go on the junction and give each arm a turn, which beats signs on every arm because a car that has actually stopped has to get going again &mdash; but an arm with a green and no car on it wastes the whole phase. A roundabout holds two cars rather than one and carries more than either, and is the only piece that can seize solid. Nine levels, each with a budget for road and a tally of pieces; nothing random anywhere, so the same roads give the same run twice. [`tests/games/test-gridlock.mjs`](tests/games/test-gridlock.mjs) plays each of the three teaching levels twice, without the piece and with it, and fails if the one without wins. |

Nearest neighbour, box, bilinear, bicubic and Lanczos are all implemented in the page rather than left to the browser's own smoothing, and filtering happens on premultiplied alpha so transparent padding can't darken the edges. | [`remove-background.html`](remove-background.html) | Cuts the subject out of an image and hands back a transparent PNG to copy or save, cropped to what's left by default. A backdrop that the border ring says is one flat colour, or a fake-transparency checkerboard, is flooded away from the edges inwards, so colour walled in by an outline — the white inside a line drawing — survives; the flood is what comes up first on every image. Behind it a segmentation model runs locally: ISNet through [@imgly/background-removal](https://github.com/imgly/background-removal-js), bundled into [`vendor/`](vendor/) (~80 MB of weights on first use), or, on request, [BiRefNet-lite](https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/BiRefNet_lite-ONNX) through [transformers.js](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers.js), which is sharper on hair and fur and is fetched from jsDelivr and Hugging Face (~224 MB) rather than kept in the repo. Weights are cached by the browser; the image itself never leaves the page. |

The pages go in as pages rather than as pictures of pages: each is carried across as a form object with its own resources, so the text stays selectable, vector art still prints at the plotter's resolution, and a hundred pages of line work come out at kilobytes rather than megabytes. Every page of everything dropped in becomes a thumbnail you can drag into order, delete, duplicate, rotate or push to either end, and the pile saves as a single PDF. | [`rain-machine.html`](rain-machine.html) | Rain built in the page, with a map of the weather making it. You are the dot in the middle of three kilometres, and the cloud on the map is not decoration &mdash; the audio reads it. Thickness directly overhead sets how fast drops land on whatever is above your head; everything further out becomes a wash with the top rolled off, because a kilometre of air absorbs the high frequencies first. So you can stand in a gap and hear it hammering half a mile away: sparse ticks close by over a dense, dull roar in the distance. A drop is an impact rather than a plink &mdash; a noise burst a few milliseconds long fired into a bank of damped resonators that stays up for the whole session, since the panel is a fixed object and only the hit changes. The banks are the research figures as tuning seeds: a roof at 170, 290, 470, 760 and 1,180&nbsp;Hz, a windshield at 650, 1,150, 1,900 and 3,100, side glass at 850, 1,550 and 2,600, each with its own decay, which is the difference between a hollow knock, a dry tick and a light lateral tap; side glass gets a bank per side so a drop lands where it landed rather than the whole surface swinging. Drops arrive as a Poisson stream, `-ln(1 - r) / rate`, because an evenly jittered gap sounds mechanical, and strength is `0.1 + 0.9 r&sup3;` so small ones are common and heavy ones rare &mdash; with size, contact time and colour moving together rather than rolled independently. Heavier rain is therefore more drops as well as bigger ones, and drop size is its own control so turning the rain up does not silently fatten every drop. ## Theme

All pages share [`theme.css`](theme.css): a monochromatic glow on true OLED black,
set in Lexend Deca (falling back to Helvetica). The one other face is `--mono`, a
system monospace stack for text that is literally a command or a column of figures.

Two custom properties at the top of the file drive every colour in the repo:

```css
:root {
  --sat: 0;    /* 0 = white glow · 1 = full colour */
  --h: 288;    /* which colour, once --sat is above 0 */
}
```

`--sat` is the master. At `0` — the default — hue drops out entirely and the
accent is white. Raise it and `--h` starts to show; `0.35` gives a faint wash,
`1` gives full neon. Hues that work well:

| `--h` | |
| --- | --- |
| `288` | Violet-magenta |
| `190` | Cyan |
| `330` | Hot pink |
| `155` | Acid green |
| `40` | Amber |

Saturation is scaled by `--sat` throughout, and accents brighten toward white as
colour drains out, so they stay accents instead of collapsing into mid-grey.

Every page links `theme.css` in `<head>` and keeps only layout and sizing in its
own `<style>` block — no page declares a colour or a typeface. Shared components
(`.card`, `.dropzone`, `.seg`, `.swatch`, `button`, form controls) are styled once
in the theme, so a new page inherits the look with almost no CSS of its own.

Canvas drawing can't read CSS, so a page that draws its own pixels —
`anything-to-image.html`, when it renders text or a link card — pulls the
resolved colours out with `getComputedStyle` and paints with those, so what it
produces matches the page whatever `--sat` and `--h` are set to.

## Running locally

No build step, no dependencies to install. Either open a file directly, or serve the
folder so relative links behave:

```
python3 -m http.server
```

Then visit `http://localhost:8000`.

Four tools need the network the first time they run — `gifuct-js` for GIF decoding
and `gif.js` for encoding, both pulled from a CDN, the ONNX weights behind
`remove-background.html`, and `pdf.js`, which `document-viewer.html` fetches only
when you actually open a PDF. Everything else works offline, `document-viewer.html`
included for every other kind of file it opens.

The two pages that read words off a picture — `images-to-pdf.html` and
`pdf-ocr.html` — have to be served rather than opened off the filesystem,
because a browser will not start a worker from a `file://` folder. Both say so
rather than failing quietly. Neither needs the network: the engine and the
training data are in [`vendor/`](vendor/).

`speed-reader.html` needs it only when you hand it a link, and only to fetch that
link: the page itself, the article extraction and everything after it run offline.
Pasted text and dropped files never touch the network.

`remove-background.html` is the one page that isn't self-contained: it imports
[`vendor/imgly-background-removal.mjs`](vendor/), a bundled copy of an AGPL-3.0
library. It is bundled rather than pulled from a CDN because the package has a peer
dependency that a bare CDN import would have to resolve by itself. The page still
falls back to a CDN if it is carried off without the repo around it. Its optional detailed
model is the exception to the bundling: transformers.js and the BiRefNet-lite weights are
loaded from jsDelivr and Hugging Face at run time, because the weights are too big to commit.

## Tests

The pages have no build step, but they do have tests: Playwright suites that open
each page in a real browser and check what it actually did, in
[`tests/`](tests/).

```
node tests/run.mjs              # all of them
node tests/run.mjs rain noise   # the ones whose name contains either
```

It starts the server they point at and the caption stub, runs each suite and adds
up the result. [`tests/README.md`](tests/README.md) says what it needs and what
each suite covers; from an empty checkout,
`npm i -D playwright && npx playwright install chromium` is enough.

Checking the saved bytes matters more here than it looks. A page that builds a PDF
can produce a file that opens fine and has quietly rasterised every page; one that
generates audio can sound right in a quick listen and click once every eight
seconds. Both of those happened. The suites reload the saved file and read it back,
and render the audio offline and measure it.

## Games

[`games/`](games/) holds the small ones, a folder each so the address is
`/games/<name>/` rather than a file name with an extension on the end. They are
pages like any other &mdash; one file, one job, theme linked from two levels up
&mdash; and they get a tile in the hub as well as in
[`games/index.html`](games/index.html), which is what `/games/` serves.

A game has to be self-contained: copy the folder somewhere else, change the one
`theme.css` link, and it should still run. So there is no shared game engine to
import, and a game's tests live in [`tests/games/`](tests/games/) rather than
beside it. [`games/MAKING.md`](games/MAKING.md) is the procedure for writing
one and [`games/README.md`](games/README.md) is the list of the ones still to
build &mdash; sudoku, solitaire, pinball, a lane runner, a few card games and
twenty-odd others.

## Adding a page

1. Drop a new `.html` file in the repo root &mdash; or, for a game,
   `games/<name>/index.html`, which gives it the address `/games/<name>/`.
2. Link the theme in `<head>`: `<link rel="stylesheet" href="theme.css">`
3. Use the shared vocabulary — `.card`, `.dropzone`, `.field`, `.status`, `.stage`,
   `button` — and the page inherits the look with almost no CSS of its own.
4. Add a tile to `index.html`, in the group it belongs to and in its alphabetical
   place inside that group. A game goes in `games/index.html` as well.
5. Add a row to the table above, and a suite in [`tests/`](tests/).

An applet is not a page: a bookmarklet goes into
[`bookmarklets.html`](bookmarklets.html) as a source block and a list entry,
and `CLAUDE.md` says what a source may and may not contain.

Keep the "one file, one job, runs in the browser" rule. It's the whole point of the
repo, and it's why any page here can be handed to someone as a single file.
