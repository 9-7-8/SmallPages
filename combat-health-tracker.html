Combat Health Tracker

One number, big enough to read across a table, with the hits and the healing kept underneath it. Type a figure straight in, step it by one, or name an amount and take it off — and it is all still there when you come back to the tab.

It remembers. The number, the log and the amount you last typed are kept in this browser, on this page, so closing the tab mid-fight costs you nothing. They are kept as a list of what happened rather than as a lump of saved markup, which is the difference between a log you can copy out and one that is only a picture of itself. Nothing is sent anywhere, and nothing is shared between two people looking at their own copy.

Bloodied means half. Give it a maximum and the states go by proportion, which is what the words actually mean at a table: bloodied at half or less, wounded under three quarters, down at nothing. Leave the maximum empty and it falls back to counting in flat numbers — bloodied at ten or less, wounded at twenty-five — which is what this did before, and which is only right for a character whose maximum happens to suit it.

It will not go below zero. Damage past nothing stops at nothing, and the log records what actually happened rather than what was rolled: at 3 hit points a hit for 10 reads as 3 to 0, not 10 to 0. If you want death saves and the negatives that go with them, that is a different tool than this one.

Enter in the amount box heals, and Shift+Enter damages, because reaching for the mouse every round is how a tracker stops getting used. The last amount you typed stays put between rounds, since most fights hit for the same handful of numbers.
