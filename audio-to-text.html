audio → text

Record something, or drop in a file, and read back what was said — a timestamp on every line and the audio alongside to check it against. Whisper does the listening, inside this tab: the model is fetched once and cached by your browser, and the sound itself never leaves the page. English, on the models trained for English alone.

It runs here. The recording is decoded in the page, mixed down to the single 16 kHz channel Whisper expects, and handed to the model in a worker so the tab stays alive while it works. Nothing is uploaded. What is fetched, once, is the model itself: transformers.js from jsDelivr and the Whisper weights from Hugging Face, which your browser then caches. Expect roughly as long as the recording itself on a machine with WebGPU, and several times that without it.

What to expect of it. Whisper is good and not infallible: it mishears names and jargon, it punctuates to taste, and on silence or noise it can invent a line of plausible text. The timestamps are the model's own and drift a little on long recordings. Check anything that matters against the audio — clicking a line jumps the player to it, which is what that is for.

A laptop, not a phone. The model is held in memory while it runs, and a quarter of a gigabyte of weights is more than most phone browsers will hand out before they reload the tab underneath you. Tiny may manage it. Anything above that wants a real machine.
