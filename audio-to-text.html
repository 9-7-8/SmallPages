Audio → Text

Record something, or drop in a file, and read back what was said as it is heard — a timestamp on every line, the audio alongside to check it against, and the finished transcript saved to your downloads the moment it's done. Whisper does the listening, inside this tab: the model is fetched once and cached by your browser, and the sound itself never leaves the page. English, on the models trained for English alone.

Copy and .txt follow the toggle, with the timestamps or without them. .md is the same text as a document; .srt and .vtt are subtitles; .csv is a row per line for a spreadsheet, and .json keeps the timings to the millisecond.

It runs here. The recording is decoded in the page, mixed down to the single 16 kHz channel Whisper expects, and handed to the model in a worker so the tab stays alive while it works. Nothing is uploaded. What is fetched, once, is the model itself: transformers.js from jsDelivr and the Whisper weights from Hugging Face, which your browser then caches. Expect roughly as long as the recording itself on a machine with WebGPU, and several times that without it.

It reads as it goes. Whisper takes the recording thirty seconds at a time, and each window's text lands on the page as that window closes — so a long recording is readable well before it has finished, and you can switch between the timestamps and the plain words while it works. The dim line at the foot is the window being decoded right now: it is provisional, it repeats the few seconds above it, and the settled text replaces it a moment later.

What to expect of it. Whisper is good and not infallible: it mishears names and jargon, it punctuates to taste, and on silence or noise it can invent a line of plausible text. The timestamps are the model's own and drift a little on long recordings. Check anything that matters against the audio — clicking a line jumps the player to it, which is what that is for.

A laptop, not a phone. The model is held in memory while it runs, and a quarter of a gigabyte of weights is more than most phone browsers will hand out before they reload the tab underneath you. Tiny may manage it. Anything above that wants a real machine.
