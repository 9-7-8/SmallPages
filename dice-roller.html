Dice Roller

Type the roll the way you would say it — 2d6, 5d6 + 2d3, d20 + 7 — and watch it land. Every die in the roll is a solid of the right number of faces, thrown across the table and left showing the number it rolled. Then throw the same roll a million times over and see the shape of what it can do.

The shapes are the real ones. Where a die of that many faces exists, that is what you get: a tetrahedron for the d4, a cube, an octahedron, the ten-sided trapezohedron with its kite faces, a dodecahedron, an icosahedron. An odd number of sides comes out as a barrel — a prism with that many faces around it and a point at each end, which is what a real d3, d5 or d7 is, because a prism with flat ends would stand up on one. Any other even number is a bipyramid, two cones of triangles meeting at the equator, which is how a d14 or a d16 is made. The d4 is read at the top corner rather than the top face, the way a d4 with numbers at its corners is.

Past twenty-four faces it is shown on a twenty. There is no honest solid with a hundred readable faces, so a d100 — or a d37, or anything else over twenty-four — is rolled properly and landed on an icosahedron with the number it rolled written on the face that came up. The other faces carry other numbers from the same range, which is decoration; the one facing the ceiling is the result, and it is the same number as the one in the breakdown above.

The tumble is decoration over a result already decided. Every die is rolled the moment you press the button, and the throw is then aimed so each one lands with that face up. Said plainly because the alternative — letting the physics decide — would make the fairness of the roll a property of the collision code, which is not a thing anybody should have to audit. Turn Throw them off and the dice are simply set down showing their numbers, which is also what happens if your system asks for reduced motion.

The numbers come from the system's own randomness. crypto.getRandomValues rather than Math.random, and the remainder is taken only from the part of the range that divides evenly — draws that fall in the ragged tail are thrown away and drawn again. Without that last step a d6 taken as random % 6 gives the low faces a hair more often than the high ones, which is small enough never to be noticed and still a loaded die.

A million throws is what you get for leaving the box empty. Type a number for any other count, up to ten million. The chart is every total that came up and how often, as a share of the throws rather than a count, so the shape is the same whether you ran a thousand or a million; the mark across it is the roll sitting on the table above. Where there are more totals than there are columns of pixels — a hundred dice have ninety thousand of them — neighbouring totals share a bar, and the caption under the chart says how many to a bar. Point at a bar, or walk it with the arrow keys, for that one's exact figures, and Show the numbers is the same thing as a table you can read or copy.

The simulation is not the roller. Rolling a hundred dice a million times is a hundred million numbers, and asking the operating system for them one at a time takes minutes. So the simulation seeds a fast generator — sfc32 — from crypto.getRandomValues and runs from there, still throwing away the ragged tail so each die stays even. It is worked in slices of twenty milliseconds, which is why the page still answers while the count climbs and why Stop stops it where it stands and charts what it has. The dice on the table above are never simulated; those are drawn one at a time from the system.

It will take anything you can say out loud. XdY terms and plain numbers, added and subtracted: 4d6 + 2, d20 - 1, 2d8 + 1d6 + 3. The X can be left off for one die. Up to a hundred dice in a roll and a thousand sides on any of them, after which the tray and the arithmetic both stop being useful. A die showing its highest face is lit in the breakdown and a 1 is dimmed, so a natural twenty reads from across the room.

It remembers. The roll you typed, whether the dice are thrown, and the last forty results are kept in this browser, on this page. Enter in the box rolls again. Nothing is sent anywhere.
