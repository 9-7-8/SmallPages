The artwork lives in this repo. The four scans the disc is built from — the disc itself, the glossy case layer, the plastic and the tape — are in vendor/cd-image/, and the display faces beside them. The original tool linked both out to an image host and a Dropbox share, which works right up until a link stops resolving and every cover made after that is a black circle in a fallback sans.

Tinting the tape used to wipe the disc. The version this came from tinted it by filling the whole canvas and then keeping only the pixels under the tape with destination-in, which also discards everything already drawn underneath, so any tape colour but white left you with a strip floating on nothing. Here the tape is tinted on a canvas of its own and that one is laid over the disc, which is the same idea without the casualty.

The picture is cropped, not squashed. It is scaled to cover the disc at whatever zoom you set and centred, so the aspect ratio it arrived with is the aspect ratio it keeps. Brightness and contrast are the canvas filter, which means they apply to the picture and not to the gloss over it.

It runs here. Nothing is uploaded; the canvas is read back into a file by your own browser. A .png keeps the transparency around the disc, which is what you want for laying it on something else. The .jpg flattens it onto white first, because a JPEG has no transparency and would otherwise come out with a black square behind it.
