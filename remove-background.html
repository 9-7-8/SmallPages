remove background

Drop in an image and the subject comes out on transparency. A backdrop that's one flat colour, or a fake transparency checkerboard, is flooded away from the edges inwards, which leaves the white inside a line drawing alone, and it lands straight away. A segmentation model runs behind it on every image, so the other cut is one click away when the flood isn't the right answer, and its edges get a second pass that snaps them to the photo and pulls the old backdrop's colour out of hair and fur. It all runs on your own machine, and nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Flat colour reads the colour off the one-pixel ring around the image and floods it inwards from the edges. Because the flood can only start at the border, colour walled in by an outline — the white inside a line drawing, the sky through a window — is never reached, and stays. A fake-transparency checkerboard baked into the pixels (the kind that comes with a “transparent PNG” that isn't) is recognised too: when the border is two greys taking turns, both of them and every blend between them are flooded, and any walled-in patch that is clearly squares of both greys goes as well, while a patch of just one of them stays. Raise the tolerance for a noisy or JPEG-compressed backdrop, lower it when something in the picture is close to the backdrop colour. It needs no model and no download, and it's what you see first every time.

Subject model is @imgly/background-removal, an ISNet segmentation model running under ONNX Runtime, bundled into this repo so the page doesn't depend on anyone else's script host. It's the one for photographs, where there is no single backdrop colour to pull, and unless you've picked the detailed model it starts the moment an image lands rather than waiting to be asked — on a backdrop the border says isn't flat it takes the view as soon as it finishes, and on a flat one it waits under the Method control for you to compare. This page asks it for the full-precision model rather than the quantized default: about 80 MB to fetch the first time, cached by your browser after that.

Detailed model is BiRefNet-lite (MIT licensed), a 2024 segmentation network that holds on to far more of hair, fur, and thin or see-through structure than ISNet does. It runs through transformers.js on your GPU where the browser offers WebGPU, and on the CPU otherwise. Neither lives in this repo: the library comes from jsDelivr and the weights, 224 MB, from Hugging Face, the first time you pick it. Your browser caches both, and once you've picked it, it's the model that runs behind every image after that.

Edges are refined after either model. A model decides its matte at about 1024 px and has it stretched to fit, so along the edge it is a soft guess that knows nothing about where the photo's own edges are. A guided filter re-fits the matte in a band along the edge so it follows the colours of the photo itself, which brings back strands the model smeared over, and blur-fusion foreground estimation then works out what colour each part-transparent pixel would be without the backdrop showing through, so hair shot against a green wall doesn't come out green-fringed. It takes a second or two and runs off the page's main thread; As cut shows what the model gave before it, to compare. The picture itself never leaves the page.
