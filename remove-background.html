Flat colour reads the colour off the one-pixel ring around the image and floods it inwards from the edges. Because the flood can only start at the border, colour walled in by an outline — the white inside a line drawing, the sky through a window — is never reached, and stays. Raise the tolerance for a noisy or JPEG-compressed backdrop, lower it when something in the picture is close to the backdrop colour. It needs no model and no download, and it's what you see first every time.

Subject model is @imgly/background-removal, an ISNet segmentation model running under ONNX Runtime, bundled into this repo so the page doesn't depend on anyone else's script host. It's the one for photographs, where there is no single backdrop colour to pull, and it starts the moment an image lands rather than waiting to be asked — on a backdrop the border says isn't flat it takes the view as soon as it finishes, and on a flat one it waits under the Method control for you to compare. The cost of that is the fetch below, on the first image of a session rather than the first time you reach for it. This page asks it for the full-precision model rather than the quantized default: it's the sharpest of the three, and about 80 MB to fetch the first time. Your browser caches it after that, and the picture itself never leaves the page.
