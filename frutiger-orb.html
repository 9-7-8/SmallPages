Frutiger Orb

The glossy bubble every piece of mid-2000s software furniture was made of: a colour, a wide highlight across the top of it and a blurred bloom sitting in the bottom on Overlay. Pick the colour, say how much of what is behind it shows through, drop a texture in behind, and save it as a PNG, a JPEG or a WebP. The canvas below is the file — there is no separate render step.

What it is doing

Straight out of yazanoo16's beginner's guide, six steps, every one of them a control above:

  1. A circle in a colour that is not fully saturated — a pure hue has no room left to brighten into, so the highlight flattens against it and the thing reads as a flat disc. The page says so when the well is pushed that far.
  2. A wide, shallow white ellipse laid across the top of it.
  3. That ellipse masked by a gradient running down from its top edge, which is what turns it from a white blob into a reflection.
  4. A second, larger white circle sitting low in the orb, under the first.
  5. Blurred — by a fraction of the orb rather than a fixed number of pixels, so the same settings give the same orb at 512 pixels and at 4000.
  6. Mixed in on Overlay, which is what makes the colour look lit from inside rather than painted over.

Both white layers are clipped to the orb. The blur in step five is wider than the circle it is blurring, so without a clip the bloom leaks a soft halo out past the edge and the orb stops looking like glass and starts looking like a sticker photographed badly.

The opacity is the orb's, not the page's. The orb is built on a canvas of its own and then laid over the background in one go, so what shows through at 60% is the texture behind it — not the highlight showing through the colour, which is what you get by fading each layer separately.

A blurred texture is drawn larger than the canvas. A blur samples past the edge of what it is blurring, and past the edge of a picture there is nothing, so a texture blurred at its own size comes out with a pale border all the way round. It is scaled up by the blur radius on each side first, which costs nothing and has no edge to find.

The colour wells are the picker from Colour Picker. Drag in the square for saturation and brightness, the strip for hue, type a hex, or open your browser's own picker from inside the panel — and fold the whole thing away again, because two of them open at once is a page that is mostly picker. The knobs keep their own place rather than working it out from the colour each time: black is every hue at once, so a picker that reads the hex back loses where you were the moment you drag to the bottom of the square.

Transparent means no colour, not no texture. The two stack: a texture is drawn over whatever colour is behind it, so ticking the box with a texture loaded turns off a layer you cannot see anyway. It is there for the orb on its own, which is what you want for dropping one onto something else.

It runs here. Nothing is uploaded; the canvas is read back into a file by your own browser. A JPEG has no transparency, so the transparent box is ignored for one and the orb is flattened onto the colour instead — white, unless you say otherwise.

Also from a terminal: node cli/run.mjs frutiger-orb --colour "#1a7ae0" --out orb.png.
