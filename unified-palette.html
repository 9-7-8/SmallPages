Unified Palette Grid

Pick a few colours that refuse to sit together, give them a shared undertone, then cross-mix them into a full palette where every colour already agrees with every other one.

Three colours in, nine out, and as many more as you want after that.

1

Start with your colours

The ones you actually want — the green you want for grass, the purple you want for a roof. It doesn't matter yet that they clash.

2

Give them something in common

Stir the same small amount of one colour into all of them. In paint that's a dab of orange in every mix; on screen it's a nudge of the same hue through everything. Keep it low — you want a shared cast, not a filter.

Your colours
With the undertone
3

Cross-mix them in a grid

Each row belongs to one colour and stays dominant along it — the purple row holds nothing but purples. Each cell takes a splash of the column's colour. The diagonal, where a colour meets itself, gives your originals back. Tap any cell to copy its hex.

4

Stretch it into range

Lighten and darken every colour in the grid and you have highlights and shadows that still belong to the same family. Pull the saturation down for the quiet parts of a picture.

5

See it on something

A palette only proves itself in use. Reassign the colours until the little house stops fighting itself.

6

Take it with you

The grid colours, and every shade you made from them if you want the lot.
