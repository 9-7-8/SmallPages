Swirl

A single centered swirl can't repeat edge-to-edge on its own — the two edges of the frame sit on opposite sides of the vortex, so their content doesn't match (this is a real mathematical fact about swirling patterns, not a bug to tune around). "Seamlessly tileable" folds the pattern back on itself at the frame's edges so they're guaranteed to meet exactly, at the cost of a more symmetric, kaleidoscope-like look within each tile rather than one clean uninterrupted swirl.

If the source PNG has a transparent margin (rounded frame, sprite padding, etc.), the swirl automatically crops in to the opaque content so that margin is never dragged into view. "Zoom" pushes in further, beyond that safety crop, for a tighter view of the image center.

Spin and ripple speed must be whole numbers of rotations for the last frame to land back on the first exactly. Turning off "perfect loop" allows fractional values but leaves a visible seam.
