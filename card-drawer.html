Card Drawer

Draw from a deck — playing cards, a tarot deck or either half of one, an Uno deck, or one you write yourself — and watch them come off the deck one at a time and go down on the table, in a line, as past, present and future, or in a ring. Hearts are red and a green Uno card is green. Underneath, the same draw made ten thousand times over, so you can see what the totals do.

What the cards are worth

The ones that are not a number of their own. Everything else counts as the number on it.

Your own deck

One card a line. A single symbol at the front is the icon on its face, = 7 on the end is what it is worth, and ×4 puts that many of it in the deck. ♠ A = 11 ×2 is two aces of spades worth eleven each. Without a value, a card counts as the number in its name, or as nothing if it has none.

Spreads

A spread is a list of places to put cards in, and it sets how many are drawn. The ones you make are kept in this browser; the file you load is read here and goes nowhere.

The cards are only a symbol and a number. There is no art on them: a face carries the icon of its suit or colour and whatever is written on the card, which is enough to read a hand across a table. The full name of each card is in the list above, where there is room for it — a tarot face shows XIII and the list says Death.

Drawn, not rolled. A draw takes cards out of the deck and does not put them back, so no card comes up twice in one draw and asking for more cards than the deck holds is refused rather than quietly repeating itself. The shuffle is a Fisher-Yates with its draws taken from crypto.getRandomValues, the ragged tail thrown away, so every arrangement of the deck is as likely as every other.

They are dealt, not thrown. Each card is lifted off the top of the deck at the back of the table, carried over in a low arc, turned face up on the way and set down in its place — the last of the flight is the slowest part of it, which is the difference between a card being placed and a card being dropped. The deck gets shorter as they come off it. Nothing bounces and nothing is left to chance: where a card is going is decided before it leaves, and the only thing the deal decides is the order you watch them arrive in. Turn Deal them out off, or ask your system for reduced motion, and they are simply there instead.

Three ways to lay them out. In a line is the default and is what it says: one row, side by side, in the order they came out, as large as a row of that many will go. Past, present, future divides them into three rows as near equal as thirds go — the outer two take a third each and whatever will not divide goes to the present, so the present is the widest row whenever they differ and the arrangement is symmetrical either way. In a ring puts them round a circle, kept a circle rather than stretched to the shape of the table, with each card turned to face out of it the way the cards in a wheel spread lie.

The cards are the colour they are. Hearts and diamonds red, spades and clubs black, on stock the colour card actually is — and the tarot suits with them, because cups are hearts and swords are spades and always were. An Uno card is its own colour with the printing on top, which is the way round a real one works; yellow prints dark, because white on yellow cannot be read. Anything else — a major arcanum, a card out of a deck you wrote — has no colour of its own and prints in whatever the page is set to, unless you gave it a suit for an icon, in which case it is red or black like any other card wearing one. This is the one thing here the two knobs in theme.css do not reach: the table, the deck and the backs of the cards are all still the page's colour.

Face cards are worth what you say they are. Everything that is already a number counts as that number; the rest — aces, courts, Uno's skips and wilds — have a box of their own under the table, and the totals and the chart follow them. The values that come set are the usual ones: ace low, jack eleven, queen twelve, king thirteen, and Uno scored the way the box says, twenty for a skip and fifty for a wild.

Spreads are a list of places. Name one, write its places one to a line, and drawing fills them in order — the list says which card went where, and so does the table underneath each card. A spread sets how many cards are drawn, so the How many box steps aside while one is picked. Load a file takes JSON: one spread as {"name": "...", "positions": ["...", "..."]}, or an array of them, or {"spreads": [...]}, which is what Copy them as JSON gives you.

The figures are under both. Under the cards: what the hand in front of you came to, and the mean, the median and the ends of it. Under the chart: the same for the ten thousand draws behind it — how many, the mean, the median, the total that came up most often, the spread either side and the lowest and highest ever reached, with the chance of doing at least as well as the draw on the table.

Every new draw is dealt ten thousand times over. Change the deck, the count or what a face card is worth and the chart under it is redrawn from ten thousand fresh draws, so it is never of a different draw than the one on the table. The simulation deals the same way this page does — a partial shuffle, no card twice in a draw — from a fast generator seeded by the system one. It remembers. The deck you picked, what you set the faces to, your own deck and spreads, and the last forty draws are kept in this browser, on this page. Nothing is sent anywhere.
