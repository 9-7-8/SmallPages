Light and material

Internal reflections is the slider that does most of the work on a cut stone. It throws wedges outward from the centre — from the termination on a raw crystal — darkens roughly half of them, lets the rest carry the vivid saturated colour, and lays a scatter of loose triangles over the top, then the same scatter again mirrored.

Outer glow off removes the halo that spills past the silhouette, which is usually what you want for an asset on a transparent background. The Glow slider still drives the bloom inside the stone. Sparkles off removes the dispersion stars and the dust around them; on a cabochon the Fire slider keeps producing the flashes trapped under the dome.

Value check strips the colour out. If the stone stops reading as a hard, brittle object in greyscale, the hues were doing the work the values should be doing.

Faceted stones build in four passes: flat planes, reflections across them, then the highlight — one plane takes pure white, the ones around it take a brighter but less saturated version — and edge shines last. Cabochon and pearl deliberately skip it: opaque, rounded material can't disperse light into facets, so it holds one main highlight and a strong local colour instead.
