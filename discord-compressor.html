Discord Compressor

A file too big for the upload box, brought under it. A video is re-encoded — a different codec, fewer pixels, fewer frames a second, quieter audio, whichever of those you want — at a bitrate worked backwards from the size you have to hit. A picture is re-encoded the same way. Anything else is deflated, and split into parts if even that will not do it. All of it in the tab: nothing is uploaded to anybody, which rather defeats the object otherwise.

    Why the size lands where it does. A video's size is its bitrate times its length, so the sum runs the other way: the limit gives the bits, the length divides them, and what is left after the sound is the picture's budget. An encoder asked for a bitrate does not hit it exactly — measured here it came out anywhere from 74% to 121% of the figure it was given, depending on the codec and how hard the footage is — so three bitrates are encoded at once off a single read of the video and the largest result that fits is the one kept. The encoding is the cheap part; reading the frames out is not, which is why it is done once rather than three times. Footage close to white noise can beat all three — static, confetti, a screen full of snow — and then it reads the video a second time at bitrates aimed with what the first read measured: twice the wait, in the one case where the alternative is a file you cannot send.

    It will not spend a budget it does not need. A 4 MB clip given a 50 MB limit comes back at about 4 MB, not 50. Re-encoding upwards cannot put back what the first encoder threw away, so the bitrate the file arrived with is a ceiling on the bitrate it leaves with, and the line under the controls says when that ceiling is the one doing the work rather than the limit.

    It writes WebM. VP9, VP8 or AV1 for the picture and Opus for the sound, because those are what a browser's own encoders will write and this page has no encoder of its own. Discord plays WebM in its own player on every platform it has. What it is not is an MP4, so a file meant to be passed on somewhere else afterwards — into a video editor, onto a console, into something from 2009 — may want converting again at the far end. H.264 is missing for the same reason: writing one needs an MP4 container, and there isn't one here.

    Reading the video is the slow part. The page steps through the source a frame at a time, seeking to each one and drawing it, because that is the only way to read a video a browser will play without a demuxer. Roughly real time on a short clip, and slower than real time on a long one at a high frame rate — a half-hour video is not what this is for. Dropping to 15 or 24 frames a second makes it quicker as well as smaller, since a frame not wanted is a frame not read.

    Twice is worse than once. Everything here but the deflate and the split is lossy, and lossy on top of lossy compounds: a clip that has already been through this once and comes back for another pass is being quantised on top of quantisation. Go back to the original each time. The same goes for a video somebody else already squeezed to fit — there is not much left in it to take.

    What it cannot read. Only what the browser can. A video it cannot play and a picture it cannot decode come out the other end as a file to deflate or split — and it says that is what happened, naming the decoder rather than filing your clip under "a bag of bytes", because a browser without H.264 is a browser that will not open most MP4s. Safari and Firefox have no VideoEncoder at the time of writing, so the moving-picture half of this page is Chromium-only; the picture, sound and file halves are not. An animated GIF or WebP needs ImageDecoder to be read frame by frame, which is the same story.

    Splitting is not compression. It is the last thing on the list for a reason: the parts are useless until somebody puts them back together, and that somebody has to be told how. The line under the parts is the command to paste — cat on a Mac or Linux, copy /b on Windows. Discord takes ten attachments a message, so ten parts is one message and eleven is two.
