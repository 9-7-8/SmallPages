bingo cards

Type the list, say how big the card is, and take as many cards as there are people — each one its own shuffle of the same list. Anything the list doesn't reach becomes a free square, starting at the middle and working out. Give it a picture, a title and two colours and it comes out looking like something, not like a spreadsheet.

100%

How the free squares land. Every card takes its own shuffle of the list, and if the list is longer than the card each one gets a different handful of it. When the list runs short the squares left over are filled in from the middle outwards — the centre first, then the squares around it, so what's left reads as a free middle rather than as gaps. On a card with an even number of rows or columns there is no true centre, so the nearest square to it goes first.

Getting it out. Print puts one card on each page, and tells the printer to keep the colours and the picture rather than stripping them the way it does by default. Save as PNG writes each card as its own file, one a second, because a browser cancels a burst of downloads fired off from a single click. Nothing is uploaded: the picture you choose is read in the page and stays there.
