Dragon Tree

Drop the Flight Rising dragon pages you have saved and get the family tree out of them. Parents sit above their children, each dragon shows its own sprite, and the colour of a lineage runs down the tree and blends where two lineages meet — mixed in a space where averaging two colours gives the colour halfway between them rather than mud.

Colour runs down the tree. A lineage gives its founders a colour, and any dragon without one of its own takes the average of its parents' — so a dragon descended from two lineages comes out between them, and the further down you go the more the two are mixed. The averaging happens in CIELAB rather than in RGB, because the midpoint of two colours in RGB is often darker and duller than either of them, and in LAB it is the colour you would have picked by eye. The original imported a function for CIEDE2000 colour difference and never called it; nothing here needs one, since this is a midpoint and not a comparison.

Generations are placed, not guessed. The original put each parent exactly one row above its nearest child, which is fine until a dragon has children in two different generations — then one of its edges runs upward through the tree. Here a dragon sits one row below its lowest parent, which is the rule that keeps every line pointing the same way. Within a row the order comes from walking up from the youngest dragon, fathers before mothers, so families stay together; then each row is nudged so parents sit over the middle of their children.

It draws the tree it has, at the size that tree needs. The original worked out how big the picture should be, wrote the number to its log, and then drew every tree at twenty inches by ten at 300 dpi regardless — so a pair of dragons came out on a six thousand pixel canvas of mostly nothing and a hundred came out unreadable. It also gave up silently above sixty-four dragons. This sizes the drawing to the tree and tells you when one is getting big rather than stopping.

The pages are read, not fetched. Nothing here touches Flight Rising: it parses the HTML you already saved, with the browser's own parser rather than a scraping library, and the sprites come from the files saved beside them. A dragon whose page you do not have is not drawn, exactly as before — it can only be known by the link on a page you do have, and that gives a number and no picture.

It runs here. Nothing is uploaded. The tree saves as an SVG with the sprites embedded in it, so the file stands on its own, or as a PNG, and the table of dragons saves as a CSV the way the original wrote one.
