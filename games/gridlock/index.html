Gridlock

Blobs make cars; blobs of the same colour eat them. Draw the one-way roads in between and keep the whole thing moving — a loop of cars all waiting on each other is the end of the run.

    How it goes

    Drag across the board to lay road. Road is one-way, in the direction you dragged: drag back along the same cells to make it two-way, which costs twice as much and gives you a lane each way. Start a drag on a blob to get cars out of it, finish one on a blob to get cars in. Right-click, or the eraser, takes a cell's roads away again.

    A car leaves its blob, finds the shortest road to a blob of its own colour, and drives. One car fits in a lane of a cell at a time, so a junction — any cell two roads arrive at from directions that are not opposite — holds exactly one car, and everybody else queues. That is where the game is.

    • Uncontrolled junction. A car crossing one while another arm is waiting creeps over it, and while it creeps nobody else can be on it. One busy arm and one quiet one is fine; two busy arms carry about half what they should.
    • Stop sign. Goes on the cell before the junction. Cars there stop, then give way to anything on an arm without one. The other arm stops creeping and gets the junction at full speed; the signed arm waits for a gap, pays for getting going again, and will starve outright if no gap ever comes.
    • Lights. Go on the junction itself, which needs at least two arms. Each arm gets the junction to itself in turn, so nobody creeps and nobody starves — but an arm with a green and no car on it wastes the whole phase.
    • Roundabout. Goes on the junction. Two cars fit on it at once and neither creeps, so it carries more than lights can. It is also the only piece that can jam solid, because two cars on it that both cannot get off is a ring of waiting.

    You lose if a blob's queue of waiting cars overflows, if a car spends too long with no road to its colour at all, or if cars form a closed ring of waiting — each one held up by the next, all the way round. That last one is the gridlock.

    1–5 pick a tool, Space starts and stops, R restarts the run, Z undoes the last thing you drew.

    What it is not

    A toy, and it leaves out most of traffic engineering on purpose. There is one car length per cell and every car drives at the same speed, so there is no acceleration, no following distance and no queue discharge rate — a green light here empties a queue at exactly one car per cell-time. Cars take the shortest road to their colour and recompute it the moment you change the road, so nobody is ever lost, nobody avoids a jam they can see, and nobody keeps going the way they were already going. Turns cost nothing, left turns cost no more than right ones, and nothing yields on a turn except at a stop sign. A blob swallows one car at a time and never fills up. Lights are fixed time, not actuated, and there is no amber.

    Drawing road needs a pointer: there is no way to lay a road from the keyboard, which the keys the tools and the clock are on do not make up for. The board animates whenever a run is going, so a level starts paused and stays that way until you ask for it.

    The simulation is a fixed step of a sixtieth of a second with nothing random in it anywhere — blobs emit on a timer and ties go to whoever has waited longest — so the same roads and the same tools give the same run twice. Nothing is uploaded; which levels you have cleared is kept in this browser.
