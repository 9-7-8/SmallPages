Approach

Aircraft arrive from every edge and will fly straight on until you say otherwise. Drag a line from one to the strip of its own colour and it will follow it down. Let two of them touch and the shift is over.

    How it goes

    Drag from an aircraft to draw its route. It turns onto the line as fast as its own wings will let it, follows it to the end, and then carries straight on for ever. Drag again to replace a route; tap an aircraft without dragging to tear its route up and leave it flying straight.

    • Land it on its own colour. A dart wants the runway marked with a dart, a cross wants the cross, a rotor wants the pad. Nothing lands anywhere else.
    • A runway only works one way round. You have to cross the near end of it pointing roughly the way it points — the dashed line out in front of the threshold is the approach, and arriving across it at an angle means going round again. A pad takes a helicopter from any direction.
    • Turning costs room. A dart is quick and turns like a bus; a rotor is slow and turns on the spot. Line a dart up early or it will sail past the threshold.
    • Keep them apart. Two aircraft closing to within the warning ring light up. Let them touch and that is the shift over.
    • Leaving is not crashing. Anything that flies out of the sector is diverted — no harm done, but it is one you did not land.

    Traffic builds the longer you last. The seed in the corner picks the whole sequence, so Same traffic again replays the exact shift you just lost and New traffic deals a different one.

    Space starts and pauses, R restarts the shift, N deals new traffic.

    What it is not

    Not air traffic control. Everything here is one flat layer, so there is no altitude, and the single thing a real controller does most — put one aircraft a thousand feet above another and let them cross — cannot be done at all. There is no vertical separation, no speed control, no holding stack, no wake turbulence, no go-around that climbs away and rejoins, no fuel and so no diversion you have to accept. Aircraft fly at exactly one speed each and turn at exactly one rate, nothing drifts off a heading, and the wind does not blow. Separation is a plain distance between two dots rather than anything anyone would recognise as a standard, and the only thing on the ground is the runway you are aiming at.

    The simulation is a fixed step of a sixtieth of a second and the only randomness is one seeded generator deciding who arrives, when and from where, so the same seed deals the same shift every time. Pausing stops the clock and you can still draw while it is stopped, which makes the game easier; nothing here knows or cares that you did. Nothing is uploaded; your best on each field is kept in this browser.
