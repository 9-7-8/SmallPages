Speed Reader

Paste something in and read it one word at a time, in place, at a pace you set. Your eyes stop moving, which is where a good part of ordinary reading time goes. The focus letter is marked and every word is hung on it, so the word lands under your eye already lined up.

Space plays and pauses, the arrow keys step a word either way, ↑ and ↓ move the pace, and Home goes back to the start. The sentence under the word is there so that when you stop — and you will stop — you can see where you are without starting again.

It does not hold the pace exactly. A long word is given a little longer than a short one, and a word that ends a clause or a sentence is held longer still, because reading punctuation at a flat rate is what makes this kind of thing feel like being shouted at. The pace you set is the average it works out to, not the gap between every pair.

What it costs. Reading this way removes the thing that makes reading reliable: going back. On straightforward prose at 400 or 500 words a minute most people lose little. On anything you would normally re-read a line of — a contract, a proof, poetry — comprehension falls off a cliff, and no pace setting fixes that. It is a tool for getting through the inbox, not for studying.
