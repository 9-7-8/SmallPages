mic monitor

Takes whatever the microphone hears and puts it straight back out of the speakers, with a meter for how loud it is and a volume you can ride. Nothing is recorded and nothing is sent anywhere — the sound goes from the input to the output and no further.

Press start — the browser will ask for the microphone.

Wear headphones. A microphone and a speaker in the same room, with the one feeding the other, is the whole recipe for feedback — the howl builds in about a second and it builds loud. Echo cancellation is on to begin with, which mostly talks the loop out of starting, and the limiter catches what gets through; neither is a substitute for headphones, and mute is one press away.

The meter reads in dBFS, where 0 is as loud as the signal can be before it clips and everything useful sits below it. in is what the microphone sends; out is that after the volume and the limiter, which is what's actually leaving the speakers. Speech wants to land around −18, with peaks short of −6. The marker on each bar holds the loudest moment of the last second and then slides back.
