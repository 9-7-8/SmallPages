mic monitor

Takes whatever the microphone hears and puts it straight back out of the speakers, with a meter for how loud it is and a volume you can ride. It runs as close to live as a browser can be talked into, and says underneath how far behind it actually is. Nothing is recorded and nothing is sent anywhere — the sound goes from the input to the output and no further.

The delay, and what's left of it. The number under the meter is the whole trip: the buffer the microphone fills, the handful of milliseconds inside this page, and the buffer the browser hands the speakers. That last one is usually the whole story, and a Bluetooth speaker or Bluetooth headphones will be a hundred milliseconds of it or more — nothing on this page can reach that, and anything wired cuts most of it. After that: leave low latency on, which asks both devices for their smallest buffers and builds the page's audio at the microphone's own sample rate so nothing is resampled on the way past; leave echo cancellation off, which is a processing chain and costs what processing costs; and close whatever else is holding the sound card. Below that sits the floor, which belongs to the devices and the operating system rather than to anything a page is allowed to touch.

Wear headphones. A microphone and a speaker in the same room, with the one feeding the other, is the whole recipe for feedback — the howl builds in about a second and it builds loud. Echo cancellation would mostly talk the loop out of starting, but it is the one control here that trades delay for safety, and it ducks a voice when it hears itself, so it starts off; the limiter rounds off anything over −3 dB at no cost in delay. Neither is a substitute for headphones, and mute is one press away.

The meter reads in dBFS, where 0 is as loud as the signal can be before it clips and everything useful sits below it. in is what the microphone sends; out is that after the volume and the limiter, which is what's actually leaving the speakers. Speech wants to land around −18, with peaks short of −6. The marker on each bar holds the loudest moment of the last second and then slides back.
