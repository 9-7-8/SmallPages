Tile PDF Pages

Lay the pages of a PDF out side by side on big sheets — n-up imposition, for printing a whole document on a plotter, pinning a storyboard to a wall, or getting sixteen pages onto one piece of paper. The pages go in as pages, not as pictures of pages, so the text stays text and the lines stay sharp at any size you print it.

The pages go in as pages. Each one is carried across as a form object with its own content and its own resources, which is what imposition means and why the result is still a PDF rather than a contact sheet: the text is still selectable and searchable, a vector drawing still prints at the plotter's resolution rather than the screen's, and a hundred pages of line art come out a few hundred kilobytes rather than a few hundred megabytes.

The script this came from had never run. It called page.draw_onto(c, matrix=…), which is not a method PyMuPDF has or has ever had. The reason it could not have one is the real fault underneath: the script read the PDF with PyMuPDF and wrote it with a reportlab canvas, two libraries with no way to hand a page to one another. Nothing short of replacing one of them was going to fix it.

It would also have come out upside down. It tracked its position by increasing y_offset as it filled the sheet, which is how you count downwards, but a PDF counts upwards from the bottom-left corner. Page one would have landed at the bottom and each new row stacked above the last, so a document read bottom to top. Here the rows run down the sheet, which is the only way anyone reads one.

A page bigger than the sheet is a decision, not a crash. The original would have placed it anyway and let it hang off the edge. Packed mode scales an oversized page down until it fits and says how many it did that to; turn that off and those pages are left out instead, which is at least honest about it.

It runs here. Nothing is uploaded. The imposition is pdf-lib and the preview is PDF.js, both in this repo, so the page works the same opened off the filesystem as it does served.
