Money & shops

Money is a bank balance in dollars now, not something you carry - though you can withdraw it as cash if you like. Shops are ordinary chests with a sign on them. There is still no buying from across the map: if you want to trade, you travel.

Open your own shop

  1. Put a chest down and fill it with what you're selling.

    Any chest. Whatever is inside is your stock, so put in as much as you're happy to part with.

  2. Place the sign on it yourself.

    On the front of the chest, or on any block touching it. It has to be you who places it - that is the moment the shop records you as its owner.

  3. Hold the item, look at the sign, and run /shop.

    The command writes every line for you, including modded ids far too long to type into a sign editor. The shop works the moment it finishes.

  4. Closing it down.

    Break the sign. The chest and everything in it stays yours.

You areRun thisThe sign ends up saying
Selling an item at $5/shop sell 5[buy]
Buying an item at $5/shop buy 5[sell]

The command is named after what you are doing; the sign is named after what your visitor does. So /shop sell 5 correctly writes [buy] on the sign. That is not a bug - do not "fix" it by running the other one, because a [sell] shop pays money out.

If you would rather write the sign by hand

You do not need to - /shop exists precisely so nobody has to - but the four lines are:

LineWhat goes on itExample
1[buy] or [sell][buy]
2The price12
3The itemdiamond
4The rest of the item id, if it was too long for line 3 — otherwise empty 

[buy] and [sell] are named for what your visitor does, not what you do. [buy] is the one you want almost always: you stock the chest, people buy out of it, and the money lands in your account. [sell] is the opposite - it pays people to hand items in, so it needs money in your account and free space in the chest, and it stops the moment either runs out.

Writing the item name. Ordinary items can be written plainly, like diamond or oak_log. Anything from a mod needs its full id - press F3 + H to turn on advanced tooltips and the id appears in grey under the item's name.

Buying from someone else

Right-click their sign. It tells you what's for sale and the price, and one right-click buys one item - there is no stack-at-a-time shortcut, so click away.

Prices are set by whoever owns the shop, not by the server. Shop around - literally.

Selling in bulk

The price on a sign is per item. A plain click trades one item; crouch and click to trade a whole stack at once. A bulk trade stops early — and says why — when the shop runs out of stock, the buyer runs out of money, or somebody's inventory is full. There is still no way to write "64 for $10" on a sign, so a bulk deal is priced per item either way.

What crouch-clicking does not fix is the price floor. Prices go to two decimals and 0.01 is the smallest, so $10 a stack of cobblestone is 0.16 each and anything you want to sell for less than a cent an item cannot be priced at all.

Trade the compressed block instead. AllTheCompressed turns a 3×3 of almost anything into one block, so one click can move a lot:

ItemPlain cobble it holdsPrice at $10 a stack
Cobblestone 1x91.41
Cobblestone 2x8112.66
Cobblestone 3x729113.91

Each tier is nine of the one below, so the numbers climb fast — and compressed blocks still stack to 64 in the chest, so one chest holds an absurd amount of stone.

Nobody can rob your shop. Only you can open the chest or break the sign, even if it is standing in the open. Claiming the chunk on top of that does no harm.

Cash, ATMs and paying people

/money shows your balance and /pay <player> <amount> sends money to someone. Neither needs you to be anywhere in particular.

The balance is the real thing, but it does not have to stay abstract: an ATM turns it into notes and coins and back again, a Banker Table does the same job, and wallets keep the cash tidy in your inventory. Cash in your pockets is not your balance - deposit it first if you want to spend it in a shop.

The server store, and emeralds

The Market stall is still here, still sells at fixed prices, and still takes emeralds - it came before the new money and has not changed. So two currencies are in play: dollars between players, emeralds at the Market and with villagers. Treat the Market as the price floor: if a player shop asks more than the Market for the same thing, you are being had.

Admin shops

An admin shop is a shop with the server behind it rather than a player. It never runs out of stock and never runs out of money — and nothing it sells comes from a chest, so nothing has to be restocked. Only an operator or someone in creative mode can make one.

They are for fixed prices that should always be available: a starter shop at spawn, a sink for something people have too much of, a reference price so nobody gets fleeced.

  1. Make an ordinary shop first.

    Chest, sign placed by you, then /shop sell 5 or /shop buy 5 exactly as above. /adminshop does not write the sign — it only changes who owns it, so the shop has to work before you mark it.

  2. Look at the sign and run /adminshop.

    That's it. The shop now belongs to the server.

  3. Or name the sign by position: /adminshop at <x> <y> <z>

    Useful when you can't get line of sight on it, or when you are marking several.

  4. Changed your mind: /adminshop off

    Hands the shop back to you, as its ordinary owner. Same two ways of naming the sign.

Sign saysWhat the admin shop doesThe chest
[buy]Sells to anyone, for ever. Stock never runs down.Stays empty. Nothing is taken out of it.
[sell]Buys from anyone, for ever. Never runs out of money.Stays empty. Nothing is put into it.

The chest is still needed, and it still stays empty. An admin shop reads nothing out of the chest and writes nothing into it, so there is no point stocking one — and no harm in the chest being there. The money the customer pays simply stops existing, and the money an admin shop pays out simply appears.

The customer side is unchanged: one right-click buys or sells one item, they are told the price and their new balance, and they are still refused if they can't afford it, don't have the item, or have a full inventory.

If /adminshop doesn't autocomplete after you switch to creative, rejoin. The game sends your client the list of commands you're allowed when your operator status changes, but not when your gamemode does. The command still works if you type it out in full; rejoining just makes the tab-completion catch up.
