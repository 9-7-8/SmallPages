It decodes once. The script this came from re-opened the video and walked it again on every attempt at getting under the size limit, which is the slow part done repeatedly for no reason. Here the frames are pulled out once at the width you asked for and kept; every later attempt re-encodes those, and shrinking only ever scales them down, which it can do without touching the video again.

It aims rather than creeps. The original multiplied the dimensions by 0.8 and tried again, over and over, which overshoots as often as it undershoots. A file's size here goes roughly with its area, so the first correction is the square root of how far out it was, and after that each attempt uses the two nearest measurements to aim at the target. It usually lands in two or three tries, and it tells you each one it made.

Three bugs in the original, for the record. Its recursive call passed duration into the max_size slot, so every argument after it shifted along and the retry ran with nonsense. It called clip.set_speed, which moviepy has never had. And original_fps = fps was assigned after fps had already defaulted to clip.fps, so the test for whether to speed the clip up was comparing a number with itself and the branch could never run.

Which format to pick. GIF is here because something somewhere will still only take a GIF; it is limited to 256 colours a frame and it is by far the biggest. Animated WebP and APNG are the same idea done later and better — WebP lossy for photographic clips, APNG lossless when you want the pixels exactly and the colours unlimited. WebM is a video rather than an animated image, which makes it smaller than all of them by a wide margin and means it needs a <video> tag rather than an <img> — that is the only catch, and it is why the other three have not gone away.

The containers are built here. For WebP and APNG the browser encodes each frame on its own — it will happily write a single WebP or PNG — and this page assembles the animation around them: the VP8X, ANIM and ANMF chunks for WebP, the acTL, fcTL and fdAT chunks for APNG. So neither costs a frame encoder. WebM is WebCodecs doing the encoding with webm-muxer packing it, and GIF is gifenc, both in this repo.

It runs here. Nothing is uploaded, and no sound is carried across in any of the four, because none of them is a format that would hold it.
