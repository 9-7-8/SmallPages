Countdown

A set number of minutes, or a date to reach — in figures that take the whole page, with a chime at the end and the clock still right when the tab wakes up.

space start and pause · r reset

What to count to

your own clock, this browser's time zone
when it reaches zero — 0 is silent
off stops dead on nought

Two different things, one page. A length is a timer: it starts when you start it, pauses, resumes and resets, and the figure is how much of the stretch is left. To a date is a date in the diary: there is nothing to start, because the moment arrives whether anyone is watching or not, so the count runs as soon as there is a date in the box and the only control that means anything is clearing it. The line under the digits says which moment is being counted to, in full.

It counts from the clock, not from its own ticks. What is left is the difference between now and the moment it is due, worked out fresh every frame, rather than a thousand subtractions that each lose a little. Browsers throttle a tab you are not looking at and a locked phone stops it altogether; come back and the figure is already right, because nothing was being counted while you were away. A length that was running when the tab closed is still running when the page opens again.

Past zero it keeps going. The box turns and the figure counts up with a + in front of it, because the useful question after a deadline is usually how far past it you are. Turn keep counting past zero off and it stops dead on nought instead. A date that is already behind you shows as overtime straight away, which is how Monday, 9am on a Monday afternoon reads.

The chime is made here, not played from a file. Struck tones from the browser's own audio engine, a fall of four. Browsers will not let a page make sound until you have asked it to, which is why the first start is also what builds it — and why a date countdown you only watched rather than started may reach zero silently. A chime that falls due while the tab is asleep arrives late or not at all; the figure is the signal to trust.

It remembers. Which of the two you were counting, the length, the date, the chime volume and whether a length was running are kept in this browser, on this page. Nothing is sent anywhere.
