Rain Machine

Rain built from scratch in the page, with a map of the weather making it. You are the dot in the middle; the cells drift past on the wind, gusts sweep through, and when lightning strikes you see the flash where it landed and watch the sound travel to you at the speed it really travels — about three seconds a kilometre — arriving when the ring reaches the middle. Step inside a car, a house, or under the ground and the same storm reaches you through what is in the way.

Nothing playing.

The map is the sound, not a picture of it. Every mark on it is something the audio engine is doing. The drifting speckle is the rain cells, and how thick it is sets how hard the noise underneath is driven. A gust is drawn as an arc crossing the map and heard as a swell through a resonant filter as it passes. A strike flashes where it lands, and the ring leaving it travels at 343 metres a second against a map three kilometres across, so a strike at the edge takes nearly nine seconds to reach you — which is exactly when the sound is scheduled. Count the ring in and you are counting the real thing.

Four places to stand, and the difference is what is in the way. Outside is the storm unobstructed. A car is a small hard box: the exterior is cut above about 1.6 kHz, there is a boom around 120 Hz from the cabin, and the rain on the roof is louder and sharper than anything outside, because it is a steel sheet a foot from your head. A house takes more of the top off and adds the slower, softer patter of rain on glass. Underground the storm is almost all gone — everything above a few hundred hertz with it — and what is left is a rumble through earth and the drips of water that found a way in, with a long tail on them, because a tunnel is a reverberant place. The reverberation is a tail generated in the page rather than a recorded one: noise under an exponential decay, convolved.

Nothing is a recording. The rain is filtered noise with individual drops scheduled over it, the thunder is a shaped burst, and the room each one is heard in is a filter chain. There is nothing to download and no loop, so it never repeats.
