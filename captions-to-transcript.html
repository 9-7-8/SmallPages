Drop a .vtt, .srt or any of the shapes YouTube hands out and get back a transcript with the repetition taken out — automatic captions say every line twice as they scroll, which is why pasting them anywhere gives you a wall of stutter. Paste a link and it will try to fetch them for you; when that is refused, it writes the yt-dlp command instead.

    The repetition is the whole problem. An automatic caption track
  is written for a two-line box that scrolls, so each cue repeats the tail of the one before it
  and the text arrives roughly twice over. The script this came from ran the file through
  grep and one sed, which stripped the WEBVTT header and
  turned each timing line into a bare timestamp — and left every duplicated line, every
  blank line and every inline <c> tag exactly where they were. What came out was
  not a transcript. Here each cue is compared against what has already been said and only the new
  words are kept, matched on words rather than whole lines so a cue that repeats half of its
  predecessor is handled too.
    

  
    Five caption formats, because YouTube uses several. VTT and SRT
  are the ones you will have; json3, srv1 through srv3 and
  TTML are what its own player asks for, and which of them you get depends on how you asked. All
  of them come in here, and the timings survive, so the output can go back out as SRT or VTT as
  well as prose.
    

  
    The link box is best effort and the command is not. A browser is
  not allowed to read another site's pages, so fetching captions means going through a relay —
  a third party that fetches the page and passes it back, which means it also sees which video you
  asked for. They rate-limit, they come and go, and YouTube increasingly refuses to hand captions
  to anything that cannot prove it is a real player: a relay that answers perfectly well can still
  hand back a page with the captions stripped out of it, which is counted here as a failure so the
  next route gets its turn. corsproxy.io was in this list until it started answering
  401 to anyone without an account. Put your own relay in the box and it is tried before any of
  them. When none of it works the yt-dlp command underneath always does, and the file
  it writes drops straight back in here — the original only ever used yt-dlp,
  which is why the original worked.
    

  
    It runs here. The parsing, the de-duplication and every output
  happen in this tab. The only thing that leaves is the link itself, to the relay, and only when
  you press the button.
